A Simple Number Sense Plan

Number sense is the feel for how numbers work. Before drills, help the learner see that 34 means 3 tens and 4 ones. Strong number sense makes addition, subtraction, and place value far easier.

  • Practice for 5 to 10 minutes.
  • Use tens and ones blocks, drawings, or coins.
  • Read numbers out loud together.
  • Compare two numbers and say which is greater.
  • Stop before the learner feels overloaded.

🫧 Bubbles Says

Break a number into parts. 47 is 40 and 7. That makes counting on, comparing, and rounding much easier.

Four Number Sense Skills

1

Place Value

See what each digit means. Example: in 256, the 2 means 200, the 5 means 50, and the 6 means 6.

2

Counting Patterns

Count by 2s, 5s, and 10s. Patterns help learners predict the next number with confidence.

Open Math Facts
3

Comparing Numbers

Decide which is greater. Example: 63 is greater than 58 because 6 tens beat 5 tens.

4

Rounding

Round to the nearest ten. Example: 47 rounds up to 50; 42 rounds down to 40.

What to Say When a Learner Gets Stuck

  • Take your time.
  • How many tens and how many ones?
  • Can we draw it with blocks?
  • Which number is bigger, and how do you know?
  • Mistakes help us see what to practice next.

What to Avoid

  • Do not rush to big numbers too soon.
  • Do not shame wrong answers.
  • Do not skip the tens-and-ones step.
  • Do not keep going after the learner is overloaded.
